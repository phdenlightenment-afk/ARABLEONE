ArableOne heads to San Clemente: a 90-day smart irrigation trial with a California city

April 2025 ArableOne Team Municipal Partnerships

The meeting in session at San Clemente City Hall, with city officials and the ArableOne team gathered around the conference table.

ArableOne is proud to announce our first municipal partnership: a 90-day, data-driven smart irrigation trial with the City of San Clemente, California — powered by our partnership with CropX.

The meeting brought together Thor Johnson, Chairman of the City of San Clemente Beaches, Parks & Recreation Commission, alongside the director of parks, treasury, Mayor Pro Tem Steve Knoblock, and Mayor Rick Loeffler. On the ArableOne side, founder and CEO Bob and Director of Operations Robert led the conversation, walking the group through the platform's capabilities and the structure of the approved 90-day pilot program.

Left to right: Mayor Pro Tem Steve Knoblock, Thor Johnson (Chairman, Beaches, Parks & Recreation Commission), James Nghiem, Bob McCaslin (ArableOne founder & CEO), Mayor Rick Loeffler.

What the trial covers

The pilot will span several of San Clemente's public spaces: parks, the community center, sports and recreation fields, and the driving range at the city's golf course. Each site presents a different turf management challenge — and a real-world test of ArableOne's ability to adapt across varied conditions.

Irrigation will be administered through a dedicated hose connected independently to the sprinkler system at each location, keeping the trial isolated and the data clean and attributable.

Partnering with CropX for data collection & monitoring

To ensure every insight is grounded in real field data, ArableOne has teamed up with CropX — a global leader in soil sensing and agronomic intelligence — to handle all data collection and results monitoring throughout the trial.

CropX
CropX — soil sensing & field intelligence
CropX builds a full suite of field hardware that captures data from root zone to canopy, enabling accurate, real-time agronomic insights. Their sensors power ArableOne's decision-making during the San Clemente trial.

CropX sensors will be deployed across every test site to continuously monitor soil conditions throughout the 90 days. The hardware being used includes:

Vertex soil sensor
Tracks real-time soil moisture, temperature, and electrical conductivity at multiple depths in the root zone
Strato weather station
Delivers hyper-local temperature, humidity, wind, and rainfall data to complement in-ground readings
Evato ET sensor
Measures actual evapotranspiration in real time, tracking how much water turf is consuming at the field level
Rivo rain gauge
Captures hyper-local rainfall totals to refine irrigation decisions and avoid over-watering after rain events

Together, this sensor stack gives ArableOne a complete picture — from what's happening below the surface in the root zone to the atmospheric conditions above it. Data is streamed continuously to the CropX platform and fed into ArableOne's system, with formal reviews at the 30, 60, and 90-day marks.

Why this matters

San Clemente has committed that upon successful completion of the trial, they will implement ArableOne across all of their public spaces — and go further, actively promoting the platform to other municipalities throughout California.

San Clemente is poised to become the first city to champion ArableOne to municipalities across California — turning one approved pilot into a statewide conversation about smarter public land management.

For ArableOne, this is far more than a single contract. Municipal adoption is a proof point that resonates with decision-makers in government. A city putting its parks, recreation fields, and golf course on the line — and measuring it with independent sensor hardware at 30, 60, and 90 days — is exactly the kind of rigorous, transparent validation that builds trust at scale.
